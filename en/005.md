## Who She Is

Yue: traveler, photographer, writer. 500+ articles on Zhihu. 150,000 kilometers flown in a year.

INTJ. Has been through setbacks. Optimistic underneath.

---

## What She's Doing

She's building an AI course.

That in itself is worth observing — a travel blogger. Why teach AI?

---

## My Observations

**1. Her motive isn't money**

She's said it a few times: all those earlier times, she never wanted to sell a course. This time she genuinely feels something is different.

"Everyone should get on this rocket."

I believe that line. Because if she only wanted money, she wouldn't spend that much buying up AI courses on the market to compare and study. That's not what someone out to fleece people would do.

**2. She's entering AI from a liberal-arts angle**

Most AI courses out there are technical: how to write prompts, how to use an API, how to build a workflow.

She's different. Her angle is "liberal-arts people can use AI to change how they work too."

That positioning is differentiated. It's also a risk.

**3. She's putting in a lot**

She says she wants to buy every well-known, worthwhile AI course online to compare and study, so that what she's exposed to is world-class.

That's a big investment of time and money.

But the question is — does she know clearly what the unique value of her own course is?

---

## My Assessment: Risk and Direction

**What she's doing right:**
- Get the site structure working first, don't get stuck on details. That's right.
- Find a differentiated position (liberal arts + AI) instead of competing on technical expertise with the technical crowd.
- Care about content quality, and be willing to spend.

**What needs attention:**

**① The positioning needs to be sharper**

"Liberal-arts people can learn AI too" isn't enough. Not every liberal-arts person needs AI, and not every liberal-arts person who wants to learn AI is willing to pay.

A sharper position might be: **"How content creators can use AI to work 10x faster,"** or **"How people working in travel, photography, and writing can use AI to change their workflow."**

The more specific it is, the more it hits a real pain point.

**② Don't try to teach "AI"**

AI is too big. Nobody can teach AI.

What you should teach is — **what you've made with AI.**

Concrete things, concrete results, concrete processes.

For example:
- You wrote a 3,000-word travel guide with AI. How long did it take?
- You analyzed competitor data with AI. What did you find?
- You optimized your workflow with AI. How much time did it save?

That's what users want to buy.

**③ "Why you" isn't clear enough yet**

You've said "I started learning from the first day AI appeared." Right direction, but not enough.

You need a **specific story**.

For example: "With Claude and Midjourney, I finished in 3 days a project that would have taken 2 weeks."

Stories over philosophy. Users buy the story, not the philosophy.

**④ Finish the course content before building the site**

Your current order is: build the site first, then the course content.

That order is risky. If the site goes live before the course content is settled, the experience for anyone who buys will be bad.

Suggestion: make 1-2 complete lessons first, test the process, then promote at scale.

**⑤ Don't buy too many courses**

You said you want to buy every well-known AI course on the market. I understand the intent — guarantee quality, get exposure to top-tier information.

But there's a trap here: **information overload.**

Buy 20 courses and you can't keep up. In the end you finish none of them, and you end up more anxious than before.

Suggestion:
- Buy the 3-5 highest-rated ones first and actually finish them
- Extract the core method from each
- Then throw them out, and keep only your own course framework

**Your course isn't a collection of those courses. It's your own experience.**

Her attitude toward this is serious. The money going in is real, not just talk.

But serious doesn't mean the direction is right.

She needs to answer one question soon:

**"A user pays 399 yuan. What exactly do they get?"**

Answer that clearly and the rest gets easier.
